# Independent review of the 12-face research (2026-10-30)

Scope: branch `claude/wonderful-carson-saxyck` at `20447bc`. I read the protocol, the night report, the
log (2026-10-29/30), theory notes, fresh-eyes review, literature review and the central code, and ran
small checks (< 30 CPU-min in total) with the project's own functions on the saved files:
`ladder_exact.verify`, `ladder.pair_energy` / `degeneracy`, `handle_add.candidates` / `reach`,
`gauss_degree.admissible_gauges` / `degree_at` / `polygon_gauge`. No other file was changed; the
check scripts are short combinations of these functions (recipe in 2.1). Labels: **VERIFIED** = checked
by me in code or data, or a short proof written out here; **PLAUSIBLE**; **CONJECTURE**.

**Main finding.** The type-0 genus-4 plateau is a Gauss-map degree (normal-class) obstruction, not an
optimizer weakness. At every saved genus-3 realization, every genus-4 map fails the project's exact
Gauss filter, and in the polyhedron's own face orientation its degree is -2 instead of -3. The best
genus-4 attempts show the same signature. Seeding level g+1 from the planes of level g therefore
starts in a provably excluded region. The ladder has to move the normals first, and the tools for that
already exist.

* VERIFIED. The Gauss-degree theorem (F7) holds on the new polyhedra: all five genus-3 realizations
  have degree -2 = 1 - g in their own gauge. This is a control at non-trivial genus beyond Szilassi.
* VERIFIED (logs). `sym` and `symimm` are 25/25 UNSAT-VERIFIED by drat-trim; `immersion` is 8/8
  TIMEOUT. DRAT certifies the CNF only, so the encoder remains the trust point. An independent
  re-encoding of at least the four three-fold cases is still advisable.

**Problems.**
1. VERIFIED. **Violation counts are not comparable between tools.** `pair_energy` counts a non-vertex
   triple point that lies on two edges of one face *once*, as a self-crossing. `ladder_exact` counts it
   *twice*: a non-simple face plus a bad pair, the bad pair being a point contact that the float test
   never samples.
   * The "2-violation" genus-4 file has 4 exact defects: faces 2 and 3 non-simple, and pairs (1,2),
     (1,6) touching at P_123 and P_126.
   * The "4 honest violations" file has 8: faces 0, 2, 3, 11 non-simple, and pairs (7,9), (1,6),
     (1,2), (6,8) at P_079, P_216, P_321, P_6,8,11.

   Every isolated point contact is induced by a self-crossing: an isolated `p ∈ ∂F_X ∩ ∂F_Y` on
   `L_XY` equals `P_XYW` on both `e_XW` and `e_YW`, so face W crosses itself. The energy is therefore
   honest, but the protocol and logs mix the two scales ("plateau 4" vs "2 of one pattern"). Count
   *events* and name the counter.
2. VERIFIED from the log. **"Dead-end maps explain the solid-first plateau" is not supported.** The
   exact-inner search started *from* the extendable genus-3 realizations (reach 66, with the penalty)
   also stays at 48 (12-35 min runs). Dead-endness itself is real and robust: no map one 2-2 flip
   away from the free genus-3 map is extendable (PLAUSIBLE, node-limited `reach`), and none was found
   two flips away. It is not the cause of the plateau.
3. VERIFIED for type 0, genus 3 -> 4 (section 2). **The ladder premise has no support**: the premise
   is that a realization of the level below is a good seed for the next level, and the data
   contradict it.
4. PLAUSIBLE. **"Sub-map realizability says nothing about the full map" is too pessimistic.** It is
   logically right, since no implication holds either way. But the Gauss degree links the levels
   exactly: `D_{M+τ} = D_M + k_τ` (2.1).
5. VERIFIED. **The degeneracy diagnosis of (iii) is a correct description but a symptom.** The numbers
   check out (shortest edge 1.0e-6, margin 8.9e-9 of the diameter), but what the search is pressing
   against is a class boundary (2.1).
6. PLAUSIBLE given 2.1. **Effort spent inside the wrong class.** LNS, floors, depth weights, more
   engines, drift in the genus-3 realization space with the genus-4 energy, and level skipping from
   the same planes all start in the excluded normal class and have no term that sees it.
7. **Literature.** No primary source was read, and I could not verify the arXiv:2609.* items or the
   Bokowski-Kevin H. Sec. 8.3 reading either. Read them before any write-up.

## 2. Diagnosis of the plateaus

### 2.1 Plateau (iii): type 0, genus 3 -> 4

All of the following facts were VERIFIED with the project code. Recipe: `R` comes from
`handle_add.candidates(T3, 12)`. Then call
`admissible_gauges(N, coherent_triangles(R), target=-3)` with `N` the normals rounded to 1e-6 integers,
and `degree_at` with `polygon_gauge` signs.

1. **Figure-eight faces.** Every non-simple face in both genus-4 files has turning number 0 and
   exactly one self-crossing. These are faces 2 and 3, plus faces 0 and 11 in the 4-violation file.
   * Face 3 has the same link as in genus 3 and is simple in every genus-3 realization, yet it is a
     figure-eight in both attempts.
   * Whitney's parity rule (crossings ≡ turning + 1 mod 2) holds on all 12,060 faces of the 1005
     imported seeds.
   * Note: in this energy a single crossing is a face with even turning number, so it cannot be
     repaired without a degeneration (fact 4).
2. **The seeds are excluded.** I tested the 10 genus-4 maps at the five genus-3 realizations.
   * In the own orientation the degree is -1 or -2, never -3. For the three extendable handles it is
     -2 at all five realizations.
   * The exact filter (all 4096 gauges, degree -3 plus positive covering) finds no gauge, with one
     exception: on the polished copy, one gauge for the reach-66 alignment of (1,2,10)-(4,7,9), and it
     is not the polyhedron's orientation.
   * At realizations A and B the type-0 genus-6 map has |degree| ≤ 3 for every gauge, against a
     target of 5.
   * At the genus-4 attempts the degree in their own orientation is still -2. The 4-violation file
     has no admissible gauge. The degenerate one has a single admissible gauge, which reverses the
     simple faces 4, 5, 6, 7, 11.
3. **Handle decomposition** (proof). `τ - A - B` is a 2-cycle: the 6 tube triangles minus the two
   removed ones form an octahedral sphere on the six faces of A ∪ B. Hence for any normals and gauge
   `D_{M+τ} = D_M + k_τ`, where `k_τ` is the degree of that sphere. If the six gauge normals lie in an
   open hemisphere `{x·w > 0}`, then `-w` lies in no cone and `k_τ = 0`.
   * At all five realizations the six normals of each extendable handle lie in an open hemisphere
     (LP margin 0.02-0.28), and direct computation gives `k_τ = 0`.
   * At the attempts the optimizer moved them around the origin, but `k_τ` is still 0.
4. **Invariance** (proof sketch).
   * For a fixed map and gauge, D is locally constant in the normals. It jumps by ±1 only when the
     three gauge normals of a triangle become coplanar while positively spanning their plane (the
     cone flips hemisphere). That is a vertex passing through infinity.
   * A face's turning number, and hence its orientation, changes only at a zero-length edge (four
     planes concurrent) or a corner at infinity. The edge directions `±n_X×n_B` vary continuously, and
     consecutive ones could be antiparallel only if their lines coincided.

**Consequence.**
* VERIFIED for (1,2,10)-(4,7,9) at all five realizations and for (0,9,11)-(3,5,10) at B and C. Every
  gauge with degree -3 reverses at least one face outside the tube, and those polygons are the same as
  in genus 3. So every continuous path from these genus-3 polyhedra to a genus-4 polyhedron passes a
  vertex through infinity or degenerates a face.
* For the plateau arm (0,5,8)-(2,6,7), degree -3 can be reached by reversing tube faces only (2-13
  gauges), but none of those gauges has positive covering at the seed normals. Here the exclusion is
  pointwise and the target is explicit: those gauges.
* PLAUSIBLE. This is the mechanism of (iii). It explains the fixed pattern, its robustness across
  engines, the degenerate attractor (edges 2-10 and 0-2 → 0 is exactly a face-degeneration wall), and
  why well-conditioned attempts are worse (6-7).

**Exact tests that settle it.**
* **T1 (seconds).** Gate every (realization, handle) arm. Record the own-orientation degree, the
  admissible gauges for target `1-(g+1)`, the hemisphere margin and `k_τ`. This already gates type 0
  out at every saved seed.
* **T2 (minutes): k-targeted drift.** Run `drift_search.py` (every step exactly embedded) with
  objective = hemisphere margin, then `k_τ = -1`. The tube vertices are not vertices of the genus-3
  polyhedron, so they may pass through infinity freely.
  * Success: a genus-3 polyhedron at which `M+τ` passes T1, i.e. a seed in the right class.
  * Failure over 8 realizations × 3 handles is evidence that type 0 cannot be climbed in-class along
    this chain.
* **T3 (≤ 1 h per arm): normals first, offsets exact.** Anneal the 12 normals on exact integer
  determinants toward an admissible gauge of `M+τ`, preferably one of the explicit target gauges,
  while staying close to the seed. Then decide the offsets exactly with
  `fixed_normals_gauge.solve_gauge` generalized to sub-maps; `results.md` reports 19-90 s per
  neighbourly instance. UNSAT for all admissible normals within a radius is an exact local
  obstruction; SAT is a genus-4 polyhedron.
* **T4: Hamming-ball OM-SAT for the sub-map.** Allow at most r of the 715 signs to differ from the
  seed chirotope, and add the degree clauses with target 1-g.
  * Lemma 5.1 carries over verbatim to sub-maps (VERIFIED, proof): an endpoint `P_XYW` of a component
    of `F_X ∩ F_Y` lies in three faces, so XYW must be a triangle, which is impossible when XY is not
    an edge.
  * For non-adjacent X, Y the predicate `IN[X;Y,Z]` is the same crossing parity without the `e_XY`
    boundary term.
  * UNSAT at radius r is a certificate.

### 2.2 Plateau (ii): genus 2 failing while genus 3 is realized

CONJECTURE: same mechanism. A failed level-g attempt can sit in the degree class of level g+1 (e.g.
-2), which makes g+1 realizable from it while g is not.
* Test on the night-3 `glad` outputs: compute the own-orientation degree of every best attempt, both
  for its own map and for the next maps.
* Prediction: failed levels have degree ≠ 1-g, and successful skips land on 1-(g+1).

### 2.3 Plateau (i): n = 8 stuck at 1

PLAUSIBLE: the plateau is genuine (you showed it is not degenerate), and it may be an impossibility.
* The parity theorem (fresh-eyes 2.2: `Σ c_X + Σ C(r_v,2) ≡ χ ≡ 0`) forbids a lone crossing and a
  lone multi-reflex vertex. So the single residual violation is either a global overlap, or it hides
  a second exact defect. Re-check it with `ladder_exact`, then run T1 (target -1) and the turning
  numbers.
* The cheap way to settle it is exact: OM-SAT on 9 elements (126 signs). Either answer is worth
  recording.
* As an engine test bench, n = 8 should be dropped, as the advisor said.

## 3. New directions (ranked)

1. **Gauss-gated, normals-first ladder (T1+T3).**
   * Why it fits: each handle must lower the degree by exactly one, the degree is exactly computable
     from the normals, and the filter plus the exact fixed-normal offset solver exist.
   * First experiment: type 0, 3 handles × 5 realizations, 20 min each (≈ 5 core-h).
   * Success: an exact genus-4 polyhedron. Failure: UNSAT for all admissible normals near every seed,
     i.e. a written local obstruction; then move to other types.
2. **k-targeted drift (T2).**
   * Why it fits: `k_τ` depends only on the six handle faces.
   * First experiment: 24 arms × 15 min.
   * Success: `k_τ = -1` at an embedded genus-3 polyhedron.
3. **Class-aware energy and logging.** Add `|D-(1-g)|` (own orientation) and the number of faces with
   turning ≠ ±1 as separate terms; log them per best attempt; gate arms in `beam_ladder` /
   `archive_climb`.
   * Success: every plateau is classified as a class defect or an in-class defect.
4. **Arm selection by Gauss feasibility over the 59 types** (night 3/4 realizations): rank arms by
   admissible gauges and hemisphere margin instead of start energy. This also tests 2.2.
5. **Genus-6 normals-first generator.** Per type, anneal normals to degree -5 with covering,
   s ≥ 13 and the κ-CSP (seconds each), then exact offsets: thousands of normal sets per night instead
   of a few.
   * Ladder normals are far from this class (|D| ≤ 3 at genus-3 normals).
   * Success: SAT. Otherwise the failure statistics are the first quantitative evidence at the normal
     level.
6. **Rank-3 SAT (impossibility, normals level).**
   * Variables: the 220 signs `χ(X,Y,Z,∞)`, with rank-3 Grassmann-Plücker relations.
   * Clauses: `DEG_W` / `COV_W` at `-N_W`, `CNT ≥ 13`, the face-winding identity with `rot_X = 1` and
     `w_X ≤ 1`, and the edge/vertex-type rules on κ.
   * The instance is tiny. UNSAT for a type would be a short, human-checkable proof that the type is
     impossible; SAT feeds direction 5. PLAUSIBLE that most types are SAT.
7. **Projective re-charting move** `H ← H·A`, which moves the plane at infinity (3 parameters).
   * It changes only `χ(·,·,·,∞)`, exactly the signs that enter the degree and the turning numbers,
     and keeps the rank-4 oriented matroid of the 12 planes. One-plane moves cannot make it.
   * Experiment: 10^4 charts per genus-3 realization; count the charts where `M+τ` passes T1, then
     anneal from the best.
8. **n = 8 exactly** by 9-element OM-SAT. It settles (i); small but publishable either way.
9. **T4 with sign-target realization.** A SAT model gives target signs; then maximize the minimum
   margin of those signs, a smooth and well-conditioned problem.
10. **Stop list** (no new runs): free solid-first runs, more black-box engines on the unchanged energy,
    floor/depth variants, drift with the genus-4 energy, closure-first `pdefect`/`sdefect`,
    asymmetric immersion OM-SAT without new clauses.

## 5. Literature and things not seen

* VERIFIED (standard): H. Whitney, "On regular closed curves in the plane", Compositio Math. 4 (1937)
  276-284. It covers regular homotopy by turning number and the crossings-turning parity; this is the
  basis of the figure-eight diagnosis.
* PLAUSIBLE, check:
  * T. Banchoff, "Critical points and curvature for embedded polyhedra", J. Differential Geom. 1
    (1967) 245-256: polyhedral curvature and critical points; your covering and s ≥ 2g+1 arguments
    are of this kind.
  * T. Banchoff, "Triple points and surgery of immersed surfaces", Proc. AMS 46 (1974) 407-413:
    triple points ≡ χ mod 2, the same spirit as your parity theorem.
  * Chern-Lashof / Kuiper tightness gives `Σ_saddles |δ| ≥ 4πg = 24π`, which equals your covering
    bound, so it brings nothing new.
* PLAUSIBLE, worth reading for face-version obstructions: U. Brehm, J. M. Wills, "Polyhedral
  manifolds" (Handbook of Convex Geometry, 1993); J. Bokowski, "Computational Oriented Matroids" (CUP
  2006).
* I know of no published obstruction for the asymmetric face version and no construction beyond
  Szilassi (7 faces) and the 8-face genus-3 examples you cite (the latter not verified by me).
* What I think the project is not seeing: the Gauss degree as a per-level and per-handle invariant of
  the ladder. So far it is used only as a genus-6 prefilter on imported seeds.
