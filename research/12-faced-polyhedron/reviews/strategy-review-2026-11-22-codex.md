## 5. C3 axis alternation

**PROVED-1.** A point fixed in a face interior fixes that face. A point fixed in an edge interior preserves its two incident faces as a set, hence individually under C3. The rotation preserves both complementary closed disks (bounded and exterior sides). Brouwer's fixed-point theorem on each disk puts one pole in each. Thus even a reflex vertex crosses the axis. Tangency would put both poles on the same side. No global general-position assumption enters. As a local check in the three independent incident-plane coordinates, the rays cycle; the three short arcs form a triangle, the three long arcs meet with degree four and fail the link test.
