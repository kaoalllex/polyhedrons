* **Uniformity (PROVED-1 proof outline, cubic scope):** triple intersections defining actual vertices must be transverse;
  nonzero edge lengths, angles at corners, and separations between nonincident compact features provide finite positive
  margins. Perturb face planes within those margins. Shared edges move on their common pair-line; multiple disjoint
  segments retain endpoint order and positive gaps. General-position failures away from these incidences are finitely

  compact separation: shared features themselves have zero distance. Symmetry need not survive. This does not split

### The non-cubic gap and the proposed perturbation lemma

**Verdict:** unproved, not disproved here. "Small generic perturbation" is not a prescription for a surface. One must
choose patches in the perturbed arrangement matching the original link on a small sphere. A bounded cell can produce
an extra component or alter a face's connectivity; neither merely choosing nor discarding it proves a disk replacement.
Keeping planar faces forbids independently moving the plane at each vertex. Local choices impose constraints on the
same global plane perturbations, so individually feasible local cones need not have a common point. An abstract
triangulation of a dual polygon is not automatically realized by a perturbation of its incident planes.

A finite list of 4-6 plane cases is sufficient only after proving coverage of the relevant link/order types, valid disk
patches for all required parameter chambers, and a simultaneous global extension. A universal local result for *every*
generic perturbation could remove the compatibility issue; an existential local result does not. No such theorem is
provided. No counterexample to the existential global lemma is claimed either. The cheapest way to publish the current
negative is simply to retain "cubic"; closing the larger gap is a separate question, not a prerequisite for that note.

**PROVED-1 (elementary defect identity).** Assume a closed orientable cellulated surface with simple disk faces, distinct
face planes, vertex degrees at least three, and every pair sharing an edge. Put C=F(F-1)/2, m=E-C, and
D=sum_v(deg(v)-3). Euler and the degree sum give

    D = 2E-3V = 3F+6g-6-E; hence D+m = 3F+6g-6-C =: s.

Thus s is 2,3,3,2,0 at (8,2),(9,3),(10,4),(11,5),(12,6), respectively. At (9,4), s=9.
Since m,D>=0, a non-cubic (8,2) object has degree <=5 and at most two exceptional vertices; at (9,3), degree <=6 and
at most three. Degree-six alone, degree-five plus degree-four, or three degree-four vertices exhaust the D=3 partitions.
At s=0 cubicity and single adjacency follow under these assumptions. This explains both the small local list and why
there can still be several globally coupled singularities. It does not prove geometric perturbability.

## 5. One new idea: defect-bounded boundary census (Untested)

**Statement:** generate the non-cubic (8,2)/(9,3) maps by contracting at most s=2/3 edges in cubic *abstract* precursor
maps, then decide the necessary concurrence/order constraints directly. This avoids the geometric splitting lemma.
Distinct from I-261029-53 (a relaxed S0 target), I-261108-08 (a second cubic census), and the existing core/ladder ideas;
all graph titles were checked. This is a specific boundary-coverage algorithm at fixed F,g, not a new construction engine.

**Mechanism and coverage sketch (PROVED-1, conditional on census coverage):** triangulate every nontriangular dual face
of a non-cubic map by abstract diagonals. Its boundary labels are distinct because each primal face is simple at the
vertex. The refinement is an orientable triangular multimap on the same F vertices, still containing K_F. A k-gon
needs k-3 diagonals; in total D<=s. Dually these diagonals are forests inside the replacement vertex disks. Contracting
them recovers the original map. Therefore start from the *full* cubic census, including repeated-triple inadmissible
maps: degenerating cubic precursors need not themselves be geometrically realizable. Check simple face boundaries,
vertex links and disk contractions; never collapse a handle cycle. This argument is combinatorial only.

**Why it might work:** the omission is parameterized by at most three contractions, rather than arbitrary high valence.
The old uniform UNSAT formulas cannot see these strata. Explicit concurrence equations and direct local embedding
conditions could exclude them without ever constructing a nearby cubic polyhedron. Uniform GP clauses must not be
reused on forced-zero minors. A general nonuniform OM encoding is optional; exact coordinates with concurrence and
sign conditions may be simpler for the surviving small list. No claim that those equations will be easy to decide.

**Cheapest falsifying test:** one session, <=1 core-h aggregate, 8 shards, starting with the two (8,2) precursor maps.
Enumerate eligible one/two-edge contractions, deduplicate oriented dart maps, and test the coverage mechanism on a
square-pyramid map obtained by contracting a split apex in a cubic refinement (a combinatorial control, not S5).
Outcome: a bounded list plus successful round trips earns a separately registered geometric feasibility pilot; empty
list would need a coverage proof before any exclusion. Stop at 1000 distinct maps or the time cap: this is a cost kill,
not a nonexistence conclusion. Any failed round trip stops the proposed enumeration until coverage is repaired.

**Decision changed:** whether extending the note beyond cubic is a small finite problem or should remain parked.
The pilot does not promise a non-cubic theorem. If it passes, first try concurrence/order contradictions on one map;
a generic nonuniform solver rewrite is not authorized. The same defect parameter suggests a structural program at
slack 0-3, but no additional structural experiment is proposed without a concrete inequality.
