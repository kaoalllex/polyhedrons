# Ten faces, four handles: divergent brainstorm

Date: 2026-11-08. Every item is an **untested proposal**, including every proposed test and expected mechanism. No mathematical or computational result is claimed. Phase 1 was written before reading existing idea titles; its 25 entries are retained unchanged when duplicates are identified in Phase 2. No external references are used.

Target supplied in the brief: an embedded closed surface in three-dimensional space, ten planar simple polygonal faces, genus four, three faces per vertex, all 45 face pairs adjacent, with 48 edges and 32 vertices. Repeated adjacencies lie on their common plane-intersection line.

## Phase 1 — divergence, before comparison

1. **Architecture: design the windows before the walls.** Describe each face by the intervals it occupies on its nine intersection lines; design the empty windows that keep distant walls apart. Glue ten planar interval diagrams into a surface, making negative space the primary design variable.

2. **Graphic statics: a reciprocal scaffold.** Construct a spatial framework together with reciprocal face data and a signed equilibrium stress. Use local force closure to generate mutually compatible planes, then route polygon boundaries through the resulting joints.

3. **Knot theory: four handles as a tangle.** Begin with a regular neighbourhood of a four-cycle spine whose crossings have prescribed over/under order. Replace its boundary patches by ten labelled planar panels while preserving the spine and the crossing order.

4. **Origami: fold a ten-panel net through configuration space.** Cut a genus-four map to a disk, treat its ten faces as rigid panels, and solve loop closure by articulated linkage methods. Choose the net and its assembly path together so collisions become motion constraints.

5. **Tropical geometry: separate every conflict by scale.** Write plane coefficients as signed Puiseux monomials; put competing intersections at different powers of a small parameter. Solve leading-order incidence and separation constraints first, then lift the hierarchy to actual planes.

6. **Algebraic interpolation: recover the object from one function.** Encode plane coefficients, incidence, and strict separation using auxiliary variables in one nonnegative polynomial whose zero set is the solution set. Restrict it to structured low-dimensional families, interpolate its coefficients from exact evaluations, and recover its real zeros algebraically.

7. **Invariant hunting: the determinant password.** Build a signed matrix from cyclic face boundaries and triple-point determinants whose rank defect identifies geometric compatibility. Search for a factor that distinguishes embeddable maps, and use its vanishing locus as the construction space.

8. **Spectral geometry: let eigenvectors draw the planes.** Assign a signed, weighted operator to the dual multigraph; take a four-dimensional spectral subspace as homogeneous plane coordinates. Tune weights to prescribe the signs of the relevant four-plane determinants and make the map emerge from the operator.

9. **Program synthesis: evolve the search method.** Search over short programs built from projective changes, panel replacements, local reroutings, and exact inequality tests. Reward programs for producing families of compatible patches, then compose the successful programs into a constructor.

10. **Graph theory: a discharging argument for spatial maps.** Work on the dual triangular map and attach geometric charges to repeated adjacencies, reflex corners, and cyclic intersection orders. Find an unavoidable local configuration and either realize it as a reusable gadget or derive a geometric contradiction.

11. **Morse theory: force a contradiction in the movie.** Sweep a generic height plane through the surface and encode births, mergers, splits, and deaths of section loops. Demand four handles, ten reusable face labels, and all pairwise adjacencies; seek an impossible event order or construct a valid movie first.

12. **Bubble surgery — deliberately crazy.** Cut all four handles into paired boundary ports and cap the ports with bubbles, solve the resulting simpler surface, then remove caps and reconnect ports. Make each cap pair carry an explicit ten-plane reconnection rule so topology returns without adding faces.

13. **Self-assembly — deliberately crazy.** Give ten rigid plates patterned edge attractions and repulsive interiors, then let an event-driven assembly system choose both connectivity and pose. Encode genus four as four conserved threaded rings that the assembling shell must enclose.

14. **Crystallography: a defect in a periodic panel crystal.** Build a periodic network of planar panels, excise a finite patch with four independent tunnels, and close its boundary by identifying coplanar patches of the same ten labels. Use a localized lattice defect to break global symmetry while retaining exact coordinates.

15. **Arithmetic geometry: grow a rational object from modular seeds.** Solve the incidence equalities over several small finite fields, reconstruct rational coefficient families, and impose the real inequalities on those families. Treat prime-by-prime solutions as a generator of algebraic families rather than as real embeddings.

16. **Robotics: navigate the chambers, not the energy landscape.** Represent a state by the order of triple points on every plane-intersection line. Cross one four-plane concurrence at a time using certified local motions, and search the adjacency graph of these chambers for a compatible surface.

17. **Game playing: adversarial panel routing.** One player routes a face boundary and the other selects the most obstructive unresolved intersection. Learn a policy for repairing the selected conflict with a short combinatorial move, retaining alternate histories instead of committing to one map.

18. **Sculpture: compose three deceptive shadows.** Draw three orthographic images with the required edge occlusions and four visible tunnels, then solve for a common ten-plane spatial object. Let controlled ambiguity in the drawings supply the choices normally hidden inside a coordinate search.

19. **Braid theory: search the order along the seams.** Treat the triple points encountered along each common plane line as ordered beads and the ten face boundaries as mutually coupled words. Search for compatible word orders first and recover a plane arrangement realizing the words second.

20. **Minimal surfaces: flatten a four-handle membrane.** Start with an embedded genus-four membrane divided into ten labelled disks, evolve each disk toward one plane, and preserve a positive separation barrier between nonincident patches. Allow boundaries to slide and labels to exchange territory while retaining the target adjacencies.

21. **Tomographic manufacturing: build the slice automaton.** Specify a sequence of planar sections whose edges have ten persistent face labels; each labelled support line must move affinely with height. Synthesize the loop splits and mergers of a genus-four surface directly in this affine-line movie.

22. **Projective geometry — deliberately crazy.** Move the troublesome part of a construction through the plane at infinity, reconnect its affine pieces there, and choose a new affine chart in which the completed surface is bounded. Search chart choices and projective panel surgeries together.

23. **Quantum interference — deliberately crazy.** Form a signed state sum over boundary routings so incompatible local attachments cancel while embedded closed routings survive. Extract a surviving monomial and decode its plane-order constraints into a geometric construction.

24. **Evolutionary development: grow ten faces as one organism.** Encode a construction by a small developmental grammar that repeatedly splits territory, bends seams, and fuses coplanar patches. Evolve the grammar and its growth schedule, selecting for the complete adjacency pattern and all four handles from the outset.

25. **Contradiction by compactification — deliberately crazy.** Assume there is no solution, normalize the ten-plane parameter space, and push every near-solution to its boundary. Classify the resulting degenerations and seek a topological obstruction to all of them being terminal, forcing an interior realization.

### Selection

The scores below are subjective priorities, not measured probabilities. Surprise **S** and chance of a cheap decisive test **C** each use a 1–5 scale. A decisive test can reject the **specified mechanism or bounded version**, without deciding the original existence question. All five selections have mark N in the title screen.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|

| 2 | 1: windows before walls | 4 | 4 | 16 | A realizable noninterference rule for a repeated seam |
| 3 | 18: shadows with depth inequalities | 4 | 4 | 16 | A projected patch with a nonempty depth interval |
| 4 | 12: bubble surgery | 5 | 3 | 15 | A labelled annular replacement with no extra permanent plane |
| 5 | 6: interpolation on a plane pencil | 3 | 5 | 15 | An exact one-parameter sign atlas |

Every test below is a proposal for one session with **at most one core-hour total**. The suggested implementations use one core and a hard **eight-minute computation cap**; the remaining work is drawing, algebra, and interpretation. No mathematical test was run in this brainstorm. An unfinished computation leaves the proposal unresolved.

### 1. Affine-line slice movie (21, with 11)

**Mechanism.** Write plane i as `a_i x + b_i y + c_i z + d_i = 0`. At height z its section is a line with fixed direction and affine offset. Search for the changing cyclic words of selected section loops, with births, deaths, splits, and mergers, before choosing a complete surface map. The proposed advantage is to prescribe four handles in the whole movie, instead of crossing from genus three to four by improving a nearly completed object. The movie must also reconstruct ten connected disk faces; loop topology alone is insufficient.

**Cheapest test.** On paper, specify one local split and its reverse using three incident face labels, a reflex sector in one of those faces, and one surrounding loop; keep the full face sectors as well as the continuation of each participating labelled section arc explicit. In a six-plane patch, assign rational plane coefficients and solve for distinct event heights. Derive the interval inequalities that prevent a nonincident section arc from entering the transition. An optional eight-minute symbolic calculation checks the inequalities between consecutive event heights, rather than sampling frames. Deliver a before/event/after transition table and one rational realization of the local transition, or a contradiction for that table. A successful patch only licenses trying a movie containing four handles.

**Kill.** Reject the chosen transition template if closure requires a section line to change direction, requires different coefficients for the same face label in different frames, or admits a forced unintended arc intersection. Reject this proposed reduction if the transition cannot be composed without recovering unrestricted global plane constraints; changing the drawing would then offer no identified saving. Failure of one template says nothing about all slice movies.

### 2. Windows before walls (1)

**Mechanism.** On `L_ij`, record where each of the two face regions is present, absent, or has boundary. Require their simultaneous presence to be exactly the intended shared edge intervals. Repeated adjacency becomes a deliberate pattern of occupied intervals separated by windows. Search for the window layout and the planar polygons realizing it together. This reverses the usual order in which planes and polygons are proposed first and unwanted overlaps are counted afterward.

**Cheapest test.** Use six abstract labels A, B, C, D, E, F, with AB edge intervals bounded respectively by the distinct triples ABC/ABD and ABE/ABF. Draw candidate simple polygons in A and B; tabulate all interval endpoints, including boundary crossings not at triple vertices. Give the gap and each edge positive length, and ask whether the C/D/E/F boundary traces can close their local arcs while preserving the gap. Try the finite set of endpoint orders for this fixed six-label patch, using rational plane coefficients, their triple points, and exact planar segment tests, capped at eight minutes. Deliver one reusable local window pattern or an exhaustive rejection of that finite order list. Additional labels and the completion to closed faces remain outside this test.

**Kill.** Reject the proposed patch if every listed endpoint order forces a crossing within a face, fills the intended gap, or needs two different positions for the same triple point. Reject the claimed usefulness of the interval representation if it needs arbitrary polygon geometry to decide even this fixed local gap: then its advertised local composition rule has not materialized.

### 3. Shadows with depth inequalities (18)

**Mechanism.** Start with a chosen planar drawing of a labelled patch, including projected crossings and shared seams. For faces nonvertical in this view, use `z_i(x,y) = a_i x + b_i y + c_i`. Seam endpoints impose linear equalities; prescribed over/under crossings impose linear inequalities. Normals and offsets vary together. The hoped-for shortcut is to use a drawing to choose useful incidence and occlusion orders before tackling spatial coordinates. Two additional views constrain the drawing afterward, rather than three arbitrary images being assumed compatible.

**Cheapest test.** Draw four projected polygonal panels with a cycle of four shared seams and at least one overlap between panels that do not share a seam. Choose rational projected vertices. Impose equality of depths at both endpoints of every seam and a depth gap of at least one at vertices of the remaining overlap regions; triangulate those regions if needed. Solve the small linear system for the twelve height coefficients, then substitute the solution over every overlap region. This is a patch test, not a closed four-face surface. Deliver either explicit depth functions for the fixed drawing or the conflicting seam/occlusion inequalities. An optional LP takes at most eight minutes.

**Kill.** Reject that drawing if seam closure forces coincident panels or contradicts every allowed occlusion order at the designated crossing. Reject the proposed drawing family if all its allowed choices have this defect. A surviving patch is useful only if the residual freedom permits another panel to be attached; a uniquely frozen patch with no compatible attachment ends this version.

### 4. Cut, cap, reconnect: bubble surgery (12)

**Mechanism.** Use temporary caps to separate the global handle-routing problem from the geometry of the permanent panels. The crucial object is an annular replacement whose patches use only existing face-plane labels, with a rule for rejoining same-labelled pieces into single disks. Solve the capped pieces and matching port parameters jointly, then remove the caps and insert the annuli. The four reconnections must be planned together; this is not a proposal to resume a one-handle ladder.

**Cheapest test.** First seek one annulus between two four-edge ports, using at most six permanent plane labels and at most four interior vertices. List admissible boundary label words and interior gluings, demand cubic vertices after attachment, and forbid distinct vertices with the same triple of plane labels. For a surviving gluing, write its triple-point equations and ask whether both ports have matching free geometric parameters and nonzero clearance. Temporary cap labels must disappear from the replacement. The bounded combinatorial listing receives at most eight minutes; the session ends with either a drawn, algebraically specified gadget or a precise local obstruction. Full four-port-pair assembly is not part of this test.

**Kill.** Reject this bounded gadget family if every gluing needs an extra permanent plane, identifies distinct triple points, or leaves a disconnected or holed final face. Also reject a gadget whose port equations force zero-width seams. A gadget that works topologically but has no geometric degree of freedom for matching does not pass. This kills only the stated port size and label budget.

### 5. Interpolate the sign atlas of a plane pencil (6)

**Mechanism.** Make the owner's single-function idea concrete on a small family. For a fixed proposed routing, express necessary equalities as `f = 0` and a chosen branch of strict inequalities as `q > 0`; introduce real slack variables with `q u^2 - 1 = 0`, and sum the squared residuals into one nonnegative polynomial. Its zeros represent that branch. Instead of globally minimizing it, restrict the planes to a rational pencil `h_i(t) = A_i + t B_i` and recover the low-degree determinant factors by exact interpolation. The hoped-for gain is an exhaustive interval search along a family, without a smooth penalty hiding narrow feasible intervals.

**Cheapest test.** Specify one rational ten-plane pencil with small coefficients and a bounded parameter interval; do not claim it covers all arrangements. Use the affine element `h_infinity = (0,0,0,1)`. Interpolate every four-row determinant of the eleven rows from five rational evaluations, allowing lower degree for determinants involving infinity. Isolate their real roots, record identically zero factors separately, and pick one rational sample in each open interval. Recover the sign word of each interval; for a fixed routing, check the polygon and pair-intersection predicates there. Initially the deliverable is the sign atlas, not a solution. Cap exact root work at eight minutes. The interval boundaries need separate treatment and are not silently included.

**Kill.** Reject the chosen pencil if a required triple intersection is identically singular or every open interval fails the chosen routing; this excludes only that pencil, routing, and nondegenerate interval interiors. Reject interpolation as a useful shortcut for this version if forming the needed predicates introduces uncontrolled branching or much higher degree than the determinant atlas captures. Do not interpret a computation cap as an empty feasible set.

### Why the remaining fresh proposals did not make the five

Modular seeds (15) currently specify no restrictive incidence equations beyond those already automatic for generic planes; a modular solution would therefore not yet target the real inequalities. Self-assembly (13) needs a useful plate geometry and interaction design before its first informative test. The determinant password (7) needs a defined matrix, the cancellation proposal (23) needs a defined cancellation rule, and compactification (25) needs a manageable family of boundary cases. These omissions lower the chance of a decisive one-session test; they do not remove the proposals from Phase 1.
