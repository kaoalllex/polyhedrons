2. **No census of the (9, 3) / (10, 4) maps exists in the sources searched.** The known censuses (Altshuler-Brehm
   1992, Lutz 2005, Sulanke-Lutz 2009) are simplicial: no parallel edges, so they miss every map with doubled
   adjacencies. We must enumerate the candidate maps ourselves (cubic maps, 9 faces, genus 3, underlying simple
   graph K9 with 3 extra edge copies; likewise 10 faces, genus 4, 48 edges, 3 extra). Carrell-Chapuy 2015 only
   counts rooted maps by genus and edges. This is an own task with an own correctness control (the K12 case: 59
   types must be reproduced, FD3). A "number of candidate maps" is therefore unknown, not small by default.
3. **Why the 9- and 10-vertex neighbourly spatial polyhedra of Altshuler-Bokowski-Schuchert 1994 do not help:** they
   are orientable neighbourly 2-pseudomanifolds (several cycles of triangles around a vertex), and they are the
   *vertex* version. Their duals are not closed surfaces. Not a source of (9, 3) or (10, 4) seeds. Consistent with
   `primary-reads-2026-11-01.md` (the 12-vertex "empty set" result is also the vertex version).
4. **The count of extra adjacencies is arithmetic, and it checks.** From chi = 2 - 2g, 3V = 2E: E = 3(F + 2g - 2);
   face-neighbourly needs C(F,2) <= E, so F <= (7 + sqrt(1 + 48g))/2. Re-derived by hand in this session for
   g = 1..6 (PROVED-1: elementary, AI-checked; matches `SPEC.md` S5 and the (9, 3) numbers in `STRATEGY.md`):

   | g | max F | E | C(F,2) | extra edge copies |
   |--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
   | 1 | 7 | 21 | 21 | 0 |
   | 2 | 8 | 30 | 28 | 2 |
   | 3 | 9 | 39 | 36 | 3 |
   | 4 | 10 | 48 | 45 | 3 |
   | 5 | 11 | 57 | 55 | 2 |
   | 6 | 12 | 66 | 66 | 0 |

   The last column counts copies beyond one edge per pair; a pair may in principle share more than two edges.
   With exactly one edge per pair, g = (F-3)(F-4)/12, so F is 0, 3, 4 or 7 mod 12 (F = 12 is the next case after
   the tetrahedron and Szilassi). The review attributes the mod-12 sentence to Roest-Vigh; not re-checked.
5. **Geometry is the open gap, not combinatorics.** The review names three levels: combinatorial existence of a
   map, topological admissibility (closed orientable surface), geometric realizability (planar simple faces,
   embedded). A map existing says nothing about the third; and duality does not carry embeddedness (Roest-Vigh,
   quote in section 7.4). The review's own summary: the classification and geometric realizability of cubic
   maps whose underlying simple graph contains K_F is the least studied part, so (9, 3) is new ground, not a
   replication.
6. **Bokowski-Kevin H. 2025 failed numerically on one of the 59 duals, not on a proof for all.** The review
   quotes "finding (unsuccessfully) a dual polyhedron of one of the 59 closed oriented surfaces". Compare
   `primary-reads-2026-11-01.md` section 1 (full text read: no embedding found for the 59 duals, numerical,
   negative). The two readings differ in scope; the 2026-11-01 read of the full text is the better one. Do not
   cite the review for this point.
7. **Checked on 2026-11-03 by this session (web, not the originals in full):** Bokowski-Schewe 2002 is

## 1. Object

A closed orientable polygonal surface embedded in R^3: all faces simple planar polygons, each edge on exactly two
faces, three faces at every vertex, no self-intersection, every pair of faces sharing at least one edge
(face-neighbourly). In the dual map faces become vertices, edges stay edges, vertices become triangles; every
dual vertex is joined to every other, with parallel edges when two faces share several edges. So the objects are
geometric realizations of orientable triangular maps whose underlying simple graph contains K_F. A simplicial
triangulation and a triangular map with parallel edges are different combinatorial categories.

## 2. Realizations known (questions 1 and 2)

| Case | Search result |
|---|---|
| 8 faces, genus 2 | NOT FOUND |
| 8 faces, genus 3 | FOUND, two non-equivalent realizations |
| 9 faces, any genus (genus 3) | NOT FOUND |
| 10 faces, any genus (genus 4) | NOT FOUND |
| 11 faces, any genus (genus 5) | NOT FOUND |

* **Mizhaev, arXiv:2609.17700 (2026-09-15).** 8 faces, genus 3, 24 vertices, 36 edges, every face a planar
  9-gon, 20 pairs share one edge and 8 pairs share two, integer coordinates. Quote: "Exact verification confirms
  that all faces are planar, the surface is closed and orientable, no unintended intersections occur".
* **Roest-Vigh, arXiv:2609.32998 (2026-09-26).** Same counts, rational coordinates, the 8 doubled pairs share
  two collinear edges. Found by a "computational geometric search", verified in exact rational arithmetic. They
  prove it is not combinatorially equivalent to Mizhaev's (Proposition 2: doubled-adjacency graph is one 8-cycle
  for Mizhaev, two 4-cycles for Roest-Vigh; automorphism group C4 versus D8; "The graphs are not isomorphic.").
* Historical status (Roest-Vigh introduction): the tetrahedron and the Szilassi polyhedron "have long been
  referred to as the only known polyhedra in which each face shares an edge with every other face". With the two
  new ones, four realizations are known (genus 0, 1, 3, 3).

## 3. Sources

Entries marked (new) are not in `README.md` before 2026-11-03.

* **Szilassi, Regular Toroids, Structural Topology 13 (1986) 69-80** (new as a row): the seven-faced toroid.
* **Bokowski, Eggert, All realizations of Moebius' torus with 7 vertices, Structural Topology 17 (1991) 59-78**
  (new): all geometric realizations of the vertex version at genus 1; an oriented-matroid application.
* **Bokowski, Schewe, On Szilassi's torus, Symmetry: Culture and Science 13(3-4) (2002)**: bibliographic record
  only; content not checked by the review.
* **Altshuler, Brehm, Neighborly maps with few vertices, DCG 8 (1992) 93-104** (new): lists neighbourly maps with
  Euler characteristic > -10 ("all the neighborly maps with Euler characteristic X > -10"). Simplicial complexes
  only, so no parallel edges: not a census of our maps.
* **Altshuler, Bokowski, Schuchert, Spatial polyhedra without diagonals, Israel J. Math. 86 (1994) 373-396**
  (new): 9 vertices; "orientable neighborly 2-pseudomanifolds with 9 vertices". Pseudomanifolds, see note 3.
* **Altshuler, Bokowski, Schuchert, Sphere systems and neighborly spatial polyhedra with 10 vertices** (new):
  TU Darmstadt preprint 1993, published 1994 in Rend. Circ. Mat. Palermo Suppl. II 2. Per the 2025 survey: four
  pinched spheres among the examples.
* **Altshuler, Bokowski, Schuchert, Neighborly 2-manifolds with 12 vertices, JCTA 75 (1996) 148-162**: the 59
  orientable neighbourly genus-6 maps (already `abs1996`).
* **Brehm, A maximally symmetric polyhedron of genus 3 with 10 vertices, Mathematika 34 (1987) 237-242** (new):
  10 vertices, not 10 faces; not a result for us.
* **McMullen, Schulz, Wills, Equivelar polyhedral manifolds in E^3, Israel J. Math. 41 (1982) 331-346, and
  Polyhedral 2-manifolds in E^3 with unusually large genus, Israel J. Math. 46 (1983) 127-144** (already
  `msw1983`): general realization methods. The 1983 construction reaches genus 4097 with 4096 polygons (quoted by
  the review from Arseneva et al. 2024; the originals were not read).
* **Schulte, Wills, A polyhedral realization of Felix Klein's map {3,7}_8, J. London Math. Soc. 32 (1985)
  539-547** (already `schulte-wills1985`): regular map of genus 3; method background only.
* **Grunbaum, Szilassi, Geometric realizations of special toroidal complexes, Contributions to Discrete
  Mathematics 4(1) (2009), doi:10.55016/ojs/cdm.v4i1.61986** (new): "If all faces of a toroidal polyhedron are
  k-gons, what are the possible numbers n of faces?" Special toroidal polyhedra, not a general classification.
* **Arseneva et al., Adjacency graphs of polyhedral surfaces, DCG 71 (2024) 1429-1455** (already `arseneva2024`):
  every graph is the adjacency graph of a polyhedral surface with arbitrary polygonal cells. The class is far
  wider than ours (no closed 2-manifold, no three faces per vertex, no fixed genus), so this does not transfer.
* **Bokowski, Kevin H., Symmetry 17(4) (2025) 622, doi:10.3390/sym17040622** (already `bokowski2025`): survey of
  polyhedral embeddings of triangular regular maps and neighbourly spatial polyhedra; Section 7.4 treats
  neighbourly pseudo-manifolds with 9 and 10 vertices ("The polyhedra in all these cases are orientable
  neighborly 2-pseudomanifolds"; "There are several circular sequences of triangles around a vertex").
* **Lutz, Enumeration and random realization of triangulated surfaces, arXiv:math/0506316** (new): all
  triangulated surfaces with 9 and 10 vertices. Simplicial only.
* **Sulanke, Lutz, Isomorphism-free lexicographic enumeration of triangulated surfaces and 3-manifolds,
  arXiv:math/0610022, Eur. J. Comb. 30 (2009), doi:10.1016/j.ejc.2008.12.016** (new): all triangulated surfaces
  with 11 and 12 vertices. Simplicial only.
* **Carrell, Chapuy, Simple recurrence formulas to count maps on orientable surfaces, arXiv:1402.6300, JCTA 133
  (2015), doi:10.1016/j.jcta.2015.01.005** (new): counts rooted orientable maps by edges and genus, loops and
  multiple edges allowed. A basis for an own enumerator, not a census.

## 4. NOT FOUND list

1. A face-neighbourly closed orientable polyhedral surface with 9, 10 or 11 faces, or with (8, 2), (9, 3), (10, 4),
   (11, 5).
2. An isomorphism-free census of orientable triangular maps with genus 3, V = 9, E = 39 and underlying simple
   graph K9, or genus 4, V = 10, E = 48 and underlying simple graph K10.
3. A settled literature area that classifies these objects under the name "maximal face-neighbourly polyhedra".

## 5. Review's assessment

Well studied: strict combinatorial neighbourliness (triangulations with complete 1-skeleton), the 59 genus-6
triangulations, general polyhedral realization methods, small-vertex realization spaces, pseudo-manifolds
without diagonals on 9 and 10 vertices. Under-studied for our question: geometric realization of triangular maps
with multiple edges; classification of multigraph triangular maps with underlying K_F; the link between
topological admissibility of a map and embeddability of its dual object; the systematic study of the 9- and
10-face cases. Conclusion: the literature does not close the 9- and 10-face problem.

Relevant quote (Roest-Vigh, on why vertex results do not transfer): "His result does not transfer to the face
version, because the dual of an embedded polyhedron need not be embedded."
